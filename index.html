Standard for 2-Group Comparison (A vs B) Statistical Decision Rule

Calculates whether the difference between your prototype and the old/manual method is statistically significant, or if it could have happened by pure luck.

Statistical Formula & Decision Benchmark:
t = (x̄₁ - x̄₂) / √( (s₁²/n₁) + (s₂²/n₂) ) | Reject H₀ if p < 0.05 (95% Confidence)

Simple Rule: If the p-value is less than 0.05, there is less than a 5% chance the improvement was an accident—proving your prototype works!

How to State Your Hypotheses (Simple English):
• H₀ (Null Hypothesis): "There is no significant difference between the prototype and the baseline (p ≥ 0.05)."
• Hₐ (Alternative Hypothesis): "The prototype produces a statistically significant improvement over the baseline (p < 0.05)."
Real-World Example: Testing 30 potted plants watered by smart Arduino sensors against 30 plants watered manually. A two-sample t-test yielding t = 4.82 (p < 0.001) proves the water-saving difference is scientifically real.

State both predictions in clear, simple English. What happens if your prototype fails (H₀)? What happens if it succeeds (Hₐ)?

Example: H₀: There is no significant difference in soil moisture between the automated drip system and manual watering (p ≥ 0.05). Hₐ: The automated drip system maintains significantly higher moisture stability than manual watering (p < 0.05).
