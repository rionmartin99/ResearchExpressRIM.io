Find the Real Problem

Pick a local topic in Kidapawan City for your Robotics and Intelligent Machines (RIM) research and find out why it is happening.

Pro Tips for this Phase

  • Topic: Choose a problem you can physically observe in Kidapawan City (e.g., local farming challenges). Avoid broad global issues.
  • Tools: Use research databases like Google Scholar and tools like Google Notebook (NotebookLM) to refine your statements and identify precise gaps.
  • Evidence: Your gap must be based on a real, cited reference. You must provide the author, title, and year.
  • Gap Types: A Research Gap means missing knowledge in current literature. A Technological Gap means a physical or technical limitation in current solutions. Focus on Technological Gaps for engineering prototypes.
  • Root Cause: Don't settle for the first answer. Ask "But Why?" at least 3 times to dig past the symptoms and find the real core issue.
  • Actionable: Ensure your specific goal is something you can realistically build a prototype for or test locally.

The Problem Formula

Fill in the blanks to make a professional problem statement.

Pro Tips for this Phase

  • Be Specific: Use actual numbers or metrics for your "Current Number" and "Target Number" (e.g., "loses 30% of crops", not "loses many crops").
  • Clear Action: Your "Action/Design" should be a tangible solution (a device, a new process, or an app).
  • Scope & Budget: Define exactly what you WILL do (Scope) and what you will NOT do (Delimitations). Also, set a realistic budget limit (in PHP).

Variables, Materials & Safety

Plan what you will test and make sure it is safe.

Pro Tips for this Phase

  • Variables: The Independent variable is the ONLY thing you change. The Dependent variable is the result you measure.
  • Controls: List everything else that must stay exactly the same so your test is fair.
  • Materials (Sense-Think-Act): Sense: Reads the environment (e.g., sensors). Think: The microcontroller (e.g., Arduino). Act: Does the action (e.g., motor, pump). Power: The battery or supply.
  • Safety First: Think about real-world hazards (electrical, sharp edges, chemical). Never plan to culture germs or molds at home (Bio-Hazard rule).

Testing & Results

How will you test your design without wasting materials?

Pro Tips for this Phase

  • Target Metric: Make sure the metric directly answers your problem statement from Phase 1.
  • Statistical Power: Aim for at least 15 to 30 tests to prove your solution consistently works, not just by luck.
  • Sustainability: Think small! Test a 1/10th scale model first or use recycled materials to save money and reduce waste.
